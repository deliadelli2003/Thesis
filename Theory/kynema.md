# Kynema-SGF Concepts

## 1. Atmospheric Boundary Layer

### Atmospheric Boundary Layer (ABL)

The ABL is the lowest part of the atmosphere, where the wind is strongly influenced by the ground through friction and heating/cooling. Kynema-SGF can simulate this region including wind, turbulence, temperature and buoyancy effects.

### Neutral ABL

A neutral ABL occurs when thermal buoyancy has little influence, so turbulence is mainly generated by wind shear. This is often used as a simplified atmospheric condition for wind-energy simulations.

### Stable ABL

A stable ABL occurs when the ground is colder than the air above it, suppressing vertical motion and turbulence. It is commonly associated with nighttime atmospheric conditions.

### Unstable ABL

An unstable ABL occurs when the ground heats the air from below, producing buoyant rising air and strong vertical mixing. It is typical of sunny daytime conditions.

### Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST)

MOST describes wind speed, temperature and turbulence close to the ground using surface fluxes, roughness and atmospheric stability. Kynema-SGF uses it as a wall boundary model to calculate quantities such as friction velocity and surface heat flux.

## 2. Turbulence Modelling

### RANS — Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes

RANS models solve for the **mean flow** and model essentially all turbulent fluctuations rather than resolving them directly. They are computationally cheaper than LES but provide less detail about instantaneous turbulent structures.

### LES — Large Eddy Simulation

LES directly resolves the large turbulent eddies while modelling only turbulence smaller than the computational grid. Kynema-SGF primarily solves an LES formulation of the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations.

### Subgrid-scale (SGS) model

An SGS model represents turbulent motions that are too small for an LES grid to resolve explicitly. It determines how these unresolved motions affect momentum and sometimes temperature transport.

### Smagorinsky model

The Smagorinsky model is an SGS model that represents unresolved turbulence using an additional turbulent or eddy viscosity. This viscosity depends mainly on the resolved strain rate and the grid size.

### AMD model — Anisotropic Minimum Dissipation

The AMD model calculates the minimum additional dissipation required to stabilize unresolved turbulent motions. It accounts for different grid spacing in different directions, making it useful for anisotropic LES grids.

### AMD model for ABL

The ABL version of AMD additionally accounts for temperature and buoyancy effects when modelling unresolved turbulence. This makes it particularly suitable for atmospheric flows where thermal stratification matters.

## 3. Wall Modelling

### Wall model

A wall model estimates the effect of a solid surface on the flow without resolving the extremely thin near-wall region with very fine cells. In ABL simulations it is commonly used to calculate surface shear stress from the wind speed and ground roughness.

### Log-law wall model

The log-law model assumes that near-wall velocity follows the logarithmic law of the wall. Kynema uses the velocity near the wall to estimate friction velocity and then calculate wall shear stress.

### Dynamic wall model for waves

This wall model represents the stress produced by a moving surface such as ocean waves without requiring the grid itself to follow every wave. It accounts for wave-induced form drag together with unresolved viscous effects.

## 4. Boundary Conditions

### Boundary conditions

Boundary conditions tell Kynema what the fluid is allowed to do at the edges of the computational domain. Available choices include periodic boundaries, inflow/outflow boundaries, walls and wall models.

### Periodic boundary

A periodic boundary connects one side of the domain with the opposite side, so fluid leaving one side effectively re-enters on the other. It is useful when modelling a repeating or effectively infinite flow domain.

### Inflow boundary

An inflow boundary specifies the fluid entering the computational domain, such as its velocity and temperature. Kynema allows these values to be constant or defined using more complex functions and input data.

### Outflow boundary

An outflow boundary allows fluid to leave the simulation domain with minimal artificial influence on the upstream flow. Kynema commonly uses gradient-based conditions so variables can naturally continue out of the domain.

### Wall boundary

A wall boundary represents a solid surface through which fluid cannot pass. Depending on the selected condition, the fluid can experience friction, no-slip behaviour, slip or a modelled wall stress.

### User-defined inflow

A user-defined inflow lets you create your own spatial or temporal profiles instead of using a constant velocity or temperature. In Kynema this can be implemented through custom velocity and scalar functions.

## 5. Atmospheric Inflow Techniques

### Precursor simulation

A precursor simulation is a separate simulation run first to generate realistic turbulent atmospheric flow. Its output can then be imposed as the inflow boundary for the main simulation.

### Inflow planes

Inflow planes contain spatially varying velocity, temperature or other variables across an entire boundary. They allow realistic turbulent structures generated elsewhere to enter the main Kynema simulation.

### Synthetic turbulence

Synthetic turbulence mathematically generates turbulent fluctuations instead of obtaining them from a complete precursor simulation. It provides a cheaper way of creating a turbulent inflow with prescribed characteristics.

### Time- and space-varying boundary conditions

These boundary conditions allow quantities like wind velocity to change with both position and time. Kynema provides tools that can generate or process these boundary-plane data instead of requiring constant boundary values.

### Sponge layer

A sponge layer gradually damps disturbances near a computational boundary. It helps prevent artificial reflections from travelling back into the region of interest.

## 6. Forces

### Gravity forcing

Gravity forcing adds the effect of gravity to the momentum equations, which is especially important for water flows and buoyancy-driven atmospheric flows. Kynema can use either the complete `\rho g` gravity term or a perturbation form that removes the hydrostatic component.

### Buoyancy

Buoyancy represents vertical forces caused by temperature differences in the atmosphere. Warmer air tends to rise and colder air tends to sink, strongly influencing ABL stability and turbulence.

### Mesoscale forcing

Mesoscale forcing introduces large-scale atmospheric effects from weather models or observations into a smaller LES simulation. It can force Kynema's wind and temperature fields toward realistic time-dependent atmospheric profiles.

### Actuator forcing

Actuator forcing represents forces exerted by objects such as wind-turbine blades without resolving their complete geometry. Kynema can use actuator disks or actuator lines and can interact with turbine models such as OpenFAST.

## 7. Multiphase Flows

### Multiphase flow

A multiphase simulation contains more than one fluid, such as **air and water simultaneously**. Kynema-SGF uses a Volume-of-Fluid approach to track the interface between the two fluids.

### Volume of Fluid (VOF)

VOF assigns each computational cell a volume fraction indicating how much of that cell contains one fluid, for example water. Values near 0 or 1 represent mostly one fluid, while intermediate values identify the air-water interface.

### Level-set initialization

A level-set field mathematically describes the initial position of an interface such as the water surface. Kynema can convert this initial surface description into the VOF field used by the multiphase solver.

### Mass conservation monitoring

Mass conservation checks that fluid is not artificially created or destroyed by the numerical simulation. This is particularly important for VOF simulations because the amount of water should remain physically consistent.

### Momentum conservation monitoring

Momentum conservation checks whether changes in fluid momentum are consistent with the forces acting on the system. It helps identify numerical errors in coupled velocity and multiphase simulations.

## 8. Ocean Waves

### Ocean-wave forcing

Ocean-wave forcing generates prescribed wave motion inside the computational domain. It allows Kynema to simulate interactions between waves, wind and structures such as offshore wind turbines.

### Linear waves

Linear-wave theory describes small-amplitude sinusoidal waves using a relatively simple approximation. In Kynema these are monochromatic waves with essentially one dominant frequency and wavelength.

### Stokes waves

Stokes-wave theory includes nonlinear corrections to linear-wave theory and therefore represents larger waves more accurately. Kynema supports different orders of Stokes waves for increasingly nonlinear wave shapes.

### Irregular waves

Irregular waves consist of many wave components with different frequencies, amplitudes and phases instead of one repeating wave. Kynema can import complex wave information from tools such as HOS-Ocean through Waves2AMR.

### Relaxation zone

A relaxation zone gradually forces the numerical solution toward a desired wave field instead of changing it abruptly. Near the inlet it can generate waves, while near the opposite side it can remove waves and reduce reflections.

### Numerical beach

A numerical beach is a region where waves are gradually damped before reaching the end of the computational domain. Its purpose is to prevent artificial waves from reflecting back into the simulation.

## 9. Verification Cases

### Zalesak disk

The Zalesak disk is a standard numerical test where a slotted shape is transported or rotated through the computational domain. It checks whether the VOF algorithm can move an interface without excessively deforming it.

### Vortex patch

The vortex-patch test places an interface inside a rotating or deforming velocity field. It tests whether Kynema can accurately transport and reconstruct complex fluid interfaces.

### Zalesak disk scalar velocity

This test combines the Zalesak interface with a transported velocity or momentum field. It therefore tests whether momentum transport remains consistent with VOF transport.

### Vortex patch scalar velocity

This is a more demanding version of the vortex test where both the interface and momentum-related quantities are transported. It verifies the coupling between the multiphase and momentum equations.

---

## 10. Validation Cases

### Sloshing tank

A sloshing-tank simulation models liquid moving back and forth inside a container. It is useful for validating free-surface dynamics and gravity-driven multiphase flow.

### Dam break

A dam-break case starts with a column of water that is suddenly released and collapses under gravity. It is a standard validation problem for rapidly moving air-water interfaces.

### Breaking waves

Breaking-wave simulations model waves becoming unstable and overturning or crashing. They are challenging multiphase cases because the air-water interface undergoes very large deformation.

### Falling/inertial droplet

A droplet case tracks a liquid droplet moving through another fluid under gravity or inertia. It tests how accurately the numerical method transports a small, curved fluid interface.
