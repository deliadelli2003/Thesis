## MOSD model parameters

The total surface stress in the MOSD model is:

```math
\tau^{\mathrm{MOSD}}_{i3}
=
\frac{1}{\pi}
\left|
(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{C})
\cdot
\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}
\right|^2
\left|
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta
\right|^2
\hat{n}_i
H
\left[
(u_j-C_j)
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_j}
\right]
+
\frac{1}{2}
c_f^{\mathrm{wm}}
\hat{u}_i^\Delta
\hat{U}_{\mathrm{LES}}^\Delta
```

It can be written more simply as:

```math
\tau^{\mathrm{MOSD}}_{i3}
=
\tau^{\mathrm{WPM}}_{i3}
+
\tau^{\mathrm{EQM}}_{i3}
```

The first term represents the pressure drag produced by the resolved moving waves. The second term represents friction and the effect of unresolved surface roughness.

| Parameter | What it represents | What it is used for | Formula or source |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\tau^{\mathrm{MOSD}}_{i3}$ | Total surface stress applied to the airflow | Represents the combined effect of resolved waves and unresolved surface roughness | |
| $\tau^{\mathrm{WPM}}_{i3}$ | Windward Potential Flow Model stress | Represents the pressure or form drag generated by horizontally resolved moving waves | First term of the MOSD equation |
| $\tau^{\mathrm{EQM}}_{i3}$ | Equilibrium wall-model stress | Represents viscous effects and drag generated by unresolved ripples and small-scale roughness | |
| $i$ | Horizontal stress direction | Distinguishes stress in the two horizontal directions | $i=1$ for $x$ and $i=2$ for $y$ |
| $j$ | Summation index over the horizontal directions | Includes contributions from both the $x$- and $y$-directions | $j=1,2$ |
| $3$ | Vertical direction | Indicates horizontal momentum transfer across the sea surface | $x_3=z$ |
| $\eta(x,y,t)$ | Instantaneous sea-surface elevation | Defines the local wave geometry and its movement | Prescribed wave-elevation field |
| $a$ | Wave amplitude | Determines the vertical size of a monochromatic wave | $a=H/2$; approximately $a=H_s/2$ when the sea state is represented by one monochromatic wave |
| $H_s$ | Significant wave height | Provides the measured characteristic height of the sea state | Obtained from the buoy dataset |
| $T_p$ | Peak wave period | Represents the period associated with the highest spectral wave energy | Obtained from the buoy dataset |
| $\lambda$ | Wavelength | Determines the physical size of the resolved wave and the required horizontal resolution | For deep water: $\lambda=gT_p^2/(2\pi)$ |
| $k$ | Wavenumber | Describes the spatial variation of the wave | $k=2\pi/\lambda$ |
| $\omega$ | Angular frequency | Describes the temporal variation of the wave | $\omega=2\pi/T_p$ |
| $c$ | Phase velocity of a monochromatic wave | Determines how fast the wave shape moves | $c=\omega/k=\lambda/T_p$ |
| $\boldsymbol{C}$ | Local horizontal velocity of the wave surface | Extends the phase velocity concept to multidirectional or irregular wave fields | $C_i=-\dfrac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}\dfrac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_i}\dfrac{1}{\lvert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta\rvert^2}$ |
| $\boldsymbol{u}$ | Filtered LES air velocity | Gives the local airflow velocity interacting with the moving wave | Calculated by the LES at the MOSD sampling height |
| $\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{C}$ | Relative wind–wave velocity | Determines the strength and direction with which the airflow meets the moving surface | Difference between the LES velocity and local wave velocity |
| $\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta$ | Horizontal surface gradient | Describes the local slope and orientation of the wave | $\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta=(\partial\eta/\partial x,\partial\eta/\partial y)$ |
| $\lvert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta\rvert^2$ | Squared magnitude of the wave slope | Controls the strength of the wave-induced pressure stress | $\lvert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta\rvert^2=(\partial\eta/\partial x)^2+(\partial\eta/\partial y)^2$ |
| $ak$ | Maximum slope of a sinusoidal wave | Checks the wave steepness and the applicability of MOSD | $ak=a(2\pi/\lambda)$ |
| $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}$ | Horizontal unit vector normal to the local wave contour | Determines the direction of the pressure force | $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}=\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta/\lvert\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta\rvert$ |
| $\hat{n}_i$ | Component of the unit-normal vector in direction $i$ | Distributes the wave-induced stress between the $x$- and $y$-directions | Component $i$ of $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}$ |
| $(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{C})\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}$ | Relative velocity normal to the local wave slope | Selects the part of the relative airflow that impinges directly on the wave | Scalar product of the relative velocity and the surface-normal vector |
| $H[\cdot]$ | Heaviside function | Activates the pressure stress only on the locally windward side of the wave | $H(q)=1$ on the active side and $H(q)=0$ on the opposite side |
| $c_f^{\mathrm{wm}}$ | Wall-model friction coefficient | Determines the equilibrium stress caused by friction and unresolved roughness | Function of $Re_\Delta$ and $z_0/\Delta$ |
| $\Delta$ | Wall-model sampling height | Defines the height at which the wall model obtains the LES velocity | In the paper: $\Delta=2.5\Delta z$, corresponding to the third vertical cell centre |
| $\Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z$ | Grid-cell dimensions | Determine the spatial resolution, wall-model sampling position and LES filter scale | $\Delta x=L_x/N_x$, $\Delta y=L_y/N_y$, $\Delta z=L_z/N_z$ |
| $\hat{u}_i^\Delta$ | Filtered velocity component at height $\Delta$ | Determines the direction and magnitude of the equilibrium wall stress | Obtained from the LES solution at the wall-model height |
| $\hat{U}_{\mathrm{LES}}^\Delta$ | Magnitude of the filtered horizontal velocity at height $\Delta$ | Used to calculate the equilibrium wall stress | $\hat{U}_{\mathrm{LES}}^\Delta=\sqrt{(\hat{u}_1^\Delta)^2+(\hat{u}_2^\Delta)^2}$ |
| $Re_\Delta$ | Reynolds number at the wall-model height | Contributes to the calculation of the wall-model friction coefficient | $Re_\Delta=\hat{U}_{\mathrm{LES}}^\Delta\Delta/\nu$ |
| $\nu$ | Kinematic viscosity of air | Represents molecular momentum diffusion and determines $Re_\Delta$ | Fluid property based on air temperature and pressure |
| $z_0$ | Aerodynamic roughness length | Represents the drag produced by unresolved ripples and small surface features | Input value or $z_0=\eta'_{\mathrm{rms}}e^{-8.5\kappa}$ |
| $\eta'_{\mathrm{rms}}$ | Root-mean-square height of unresolved ripples | Can be used to calculate the equivalent aerodynamic roughness | Obtained from measurements or a separate wave model |
| $\kappa$ | von Kármán constant | Appears in the logarithmic wall law and roughness formulation | Approximately $\kappa=0.40$–$0.41$ |
| $\rho$ | Air density | Converts the kinematic stress used in the paper into dimensional stress in pascals | $\tau_{\mathrm{Pa}}=\rho\,\tau_{\mathrm{kinematic}}$ |

### Main quantities required by MOSD

The resolved-wave contribution requires:

```math
\eta(x,y,t),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta,
\qquad
\boldsymbol{C},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{u}
```

The equilibrium wall-model contribution requires:

```math
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^\Delta,
\qquad
\nu,
\qquad
z_0,
\qquad
\Delta
```

Therefore, MOSD combines:

```math
\text{resolved wave geometry and movement}
+
\text{local LES airflow}
+
\text{unresolved surface roughness}
```
