
The wave surface is moving, so the pressure force cannot be calculated using only the air velocity.

The relevant quantity is the air velocity relative to the wave:

```math
\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathrm{rel}}
=
\boldsymbol{u}
-
\boldsymbol{C}
```

where:

```math
\boldsymbol{u}_{\mathrm{rel}}
```
is the relative air velocity;

```math
\boldsymbol{u}
```

is the air velocity obtained from the LES;

```math
\boldsymbol{C}
```

is the local horizontal velocity of the wave surface.

Therefore:

```math
\text{relative air velocity}
=
\text{air velocity}
-
\text{wave velocity}
```

If the air velocity is much greater than the wave velocity, the relative velocity is large and the pressure drag is strong.

If the air and wave move at approximately the same velocity, the relative velocity is small and the pressure drag decreases.

If the wave moves faster than the air, the relative airflow changes direction. The opposite slope may then become the windward side.

### Direction of the wave slope

The direction of the local wave slope is described by:

```math
\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}
=
\frac{\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta}
{|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta|}
```

where:

```math
\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}
```

is the horizontal unit vector normal to the local wave slope;

```math
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta
```

is the gradient of the water-surface elevation;

```math
|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta|
```

is the magnitude of the gradient and represents the local wave steepness.

The part of the relative airflow directed against the slope is:

```math
(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{C})
\cdot
\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}
```

where the dot represents the scalar product.

A large positive value means that the relative airflow moves strongly against the wave surface. A value equal to zero means that the airflow moves parallel to the wave crest.

### Local velocity of the wave

The local horizontal velocity of the wave surface is:

```math
C_i
=
-
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_i}
\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta|^2}
```

where:

```math
C_i
```

is the wave-surface velocity component in the horizontal direction identified by the index;

```math
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial t}
```

is the change in surface elevation with time;

```math
\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x_i}
```

is the surface slope in the selected horizontal direction;

```math
|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\eta|^2
```

is the squared magnitude of the total horizontal surface gradient.

For a simple sinusoidal wave:

```math
\eta(x,t)
=
a\cos(kx-\omega t)
```

the wave velocity becomes:

```math
C
=
\frac{\omega}{k}
```

where:

```math
a
```

is the wave amplitude;

```math
k
```

is the wavenumber;

```math
\omega
```

is the angular frequency;

```math
C
```

is the phase velocity of the wave.
